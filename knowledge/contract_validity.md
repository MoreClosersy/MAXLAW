# Provisions on Contract Validity in Chinese Civil Code

## General Provisions on Contract Effectiveness

Article 143 of the "Civil Code of the People's Republic of China" stipulates that a civil legal act is valid if it meets the following conditions:
1. The actor has the corresponding civil capacity
2. The declaration of intention is genuine
3. It does not violate mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations, and does not violate public order and good morals

## Circumstances of Invalid Contracts

According to Article 146 of the Civil Code, a civil legal act is invalid under any of the following circumstances:
1. Performed by a person without civil capacity
2. Violating mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations
3. Violating public order and good morals
4. Using fraud, coercion, or taking advantage of a person's vulnerable position to cause the other party to act against their true intention
5. Malicious collusion that harms the legitimate rights and interests of others
6. Violating mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations, or violating public order and good morals

Article 153 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts that violate mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations are invalid. However, this excludes cases where such mandatory provisions do not result in the invalidity of the civil legal act. Civil legal acts that violate public order and good morals are invalid.

Article 154 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts where the actor maliciously colludes with the other party to harm the legitimate rights and interests of others are invalid.

## Circumstances Where Contracts Can Be Revoked

Article 147 of the Civil Code stipulates that for civil legal acts performed based on a major misunderstanding, the actor has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

Article 148 of the Civil Code stipulates that for civil legal acts where one party uses fraudulent means to cause the other party to act against their true intention, the defrauded party has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

Article 149 of the Civil Code stipulates that for civil legal acts where a third party commits fraud, causing one party to act against their true intention, if the other party knows or should know about the fraudulent act, the defrauded party has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

Article 150 of the Civil Code stipulates that for civil legal acts where one party or a third party uses coercion to cause the other party to act against their true intention, the coerced party has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

Article 151 of the Civil Code stipulates that where one party takes advantage of the other party's distressed circumstances, lack of judgment ability, or other situations, resulting in the civil legal act being obviously unfair at the time of its establishment, the injured party has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

## Legal Consequences of Contract Validity

Article 155 of the Civil Code stipulates that invalid or revoked civil legal acts have no legal binding force from the beginning.

Article 156 of the Civil Code stipulates that if part of a civil legal act is invalid and does not affect the validity of other parts, the other parts remain valid.

Article 157 of the Civil Code stipulates that after a civil legal act is deemed invalid, revoked, or determined to have no effect, the property obtained by the actor through such act shall be returned; if it cannot be returned or there is no need to return it, compensation shall be made based on its value. The party at fault shall compensate the other party for the resulting losses; if both parties are at fault, they shall each bear the corresponding responsibility. If the law provides otherwise, such provisions shall apply.
