# Basic Principles of Chinese Contract Law

## Principle of Equality

Article 3 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that the legal status of civil subjects is equal. In contractual relationships, the parties have equal status, and no party may impose its will on the other party.

## Principle of Voluntariness (Principle of Autonomy of Will)

Article 5 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that civil subjects engaging in civil activities shall follow the principle of voluntariness. The conclusion, content, and form of contracts shall be independently determined by the parties in accordance with the law, free from interference.

## Principle of Fairness

Article 6 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that civil subjects engaging in civil activities shall follow the principle of fairness. The parties to a contract shall fairly determine their respective rights and obligations.

## Principle of Good Faith

Article 7 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that civil subjects engaging in civil activities shall follow the principle of good faith, acting honestly and keeping their promises. The parties to a contract shall act in good faith when concluding and performing the contract, and shall not engage in fraud or conceal important facts.

## Principle of Legality (Principle of Lawfulness)

The content and form of contracts must comply with laws and administrative regulations, and must not violate mandatory state provisions or public order and good morals. Article 8 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that civil subjects engaging in civil activities shall not violate the law or go against public order and good morals.

## Principle of Prohibition of Abuse of Rights

Article 132 of the "Civil Code" stipulates that civil subjects shall not abuse civil rights to harm the interests of the state, public interests, or the legitimate rights and interests of others. In contractual relationships, no party shall abuse contractual rights to harm the interests of the other party or a third party.

## Principle of Public Order and Good Morals

Articles 8 and 153 of the "Civil Code" both involve the principle of public order and good morals. Contracts shall not go against public order and good morals, otherwise, they will be deemed invalid. Public order and good morals refer to public order and good customs, reflecting the basic moral standards and public interest requirements of society.

## Principle of Relativity of Contracts

Contracts are legally binding only on the contracting parties and, in principle, do not create rights and obligations for third parties. This reflects the relativity of contracts. However, in specific situations, such as third-party contracts, they may affect third parties.

## Principle of Freedom of Contract

Freedom of contract is a basic principle under market economy conditions, including freedom to contract, freedom of content, freedom of form, and other aspects. However, freedom of contract is not absolute and is restricted by factors such as law and public order and good morals.

## Principle of Change of Circumstances

Article 533 of the "Civil Code" establishes the principle of change of circumstances. After a contract is established, if, due to causes not attributable to the parties, the basic conditions of the contract undergo significant changes that were unforeseeable by the parties at the time of the contract's conclusion, and if continuing to perform the contract would be manifestly unfair to one party, the affected party may renegotiate with the other party; if negotiation fails, the parties may request a people's court or arbitration institution to modify or rescind the contract.
