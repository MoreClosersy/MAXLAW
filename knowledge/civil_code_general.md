# Basic Provisions of the General Principles of Chinese Civil Code

## Civil Subjects
Article 2 of the "Civil Code of the People's Republic of China" stipulates that civil law regulates personal and property relationships between natural persons, legal persons, and non-legal-person organizations as equal subjects.

### Natural Persons
Article 13 stipulates that natural persons have civil rights capacity from birth to death, and enjoy civil rights and bear civil obligations according to law.

Article 17 stipulates that natural persons aged 18 or above are adults. Natural persons under the age of 18 are minors.

Article 18 stipulates that adults have full civil capacity and may independently engage in civil legal acts. Minors who are 16 years of age or older and whose main source of livelihood is their own labor income are deemed to have full civil capacity.

### Legal Persons
Article 57 stipulates that a legal person is an organization that has civil rights capacity and civil conduct capacity, and independently enjoys civil rights and assumes civil obligations according to law.

Article 58 stipulates that a legal person shall be established in accordance with the law. A legal person shall have its own name, organizational structure, place of business, and property or funding. The specific conditions and procedures for the establishment of a legal person shall comply with the provisions of laws and administrative regulations. Where the establishment of a legal person requires approval from relevant authorities according to laws and administrative regulations, such provisions shall be followed.

### Non-Legal-Person Organizations
Article 102 stipulates that a non-legal-person organization is an organization that does not have the status of a legal person but can engage in civil activities in its own name according to law. Non-legal-person organizations include sole proprietorships, partnerships, professional service organizations without legal person status, etc.

## Civil Rights
### Personal Rights
Article 109 stipulates that the personal freedom and dignity of natural persons are protected by law.

Article 110 stipulates that natural persons enjoy the right to life, right to physical integrity, right to health, right to name, right to portrait, right to reputation, right to honor, right to privacy, right to marriage autonomy, and other rights. Legal persons and non-legal-person organizations enjoy rights such as the right to name, right to reputation, and right to honor.

### Civil Acts and Agency
Article 143 stipulates that a civil legal act is valid if it meets the following conditions:
1. The actor has the corresponding civil capacity;
2. The declaration of intention is genuine;
3. It does not violate mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations, and does not violate public order and good morals.

Article 146 stipulates that a civil legal act is invalid under any of the following circumstances:
1. Performed by a person without civil capacity;
2. Violating mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations;
3. Violating public order and good morals;
4. Using fraud, coercion, or taking advantage of a person's vulnerable position to cause the other party to act against their true intention;
5. Malicious collusion that harms the legitimate rights and interests of others;
6. Violating mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations, or violating public order and good morals.

Article 161 stipulates that civil subjects may perform civil legal acts through agents. Civil legal acts that must be performed personally by the actor according to legal provisions, the agreement of the parties, or the nature of the civil legal act, shall not be performed by an agent.

## Civil Liability
Article 179 stipulates that the main methods of assuming civil liability include:
1. Cessation of infringement;
2. Removal of obstacles;
3. Elimination of dangers;
4. Return of property;
5. Restoration to the original state;
6. Repair, rework, or replacement;
7. Continued performance;
8. Compensation for losses;
9. Payment of liquidated damages;
10. Elimination of adverse effects and restoration of reputation;
11. Apology;
12. Other ways of assuming civil liability as prescribed by law.

## Statute of Limitations
Article 188 stipulates that the limitation period for filing a lawsuit with a people's court to protect civil rights is three years. If the law provides otherwise, such provisions shall apply. The limitation period shall be calculated from the date when the right holder knows or should know that his rights have been infringed and who the obligor is. If the law provides otherwise, such provisions shall apply. However, if more than 20 years have passed since the date when the rights were infringed, the people's court shall not provide protection. In special circumstances, the people's court may decide to extend the period based on the application of the right holder.

## Calculation of Time Periods
Article 199 stipulates that the periods referred to in civil law shall be calculated in Gregorian calendar years, months, days, and hours.

Article 203 stipulates that if the last day of a period is a statutory holiday, the day after the end of the statutory holiday shall be the last day of the period. If there are business hours on the last day of a period, the time when business activities stop shall be the deadline.
