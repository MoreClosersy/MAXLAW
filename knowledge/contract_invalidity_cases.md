# Analysis of Contract Invalidity Cases

## Case 1: Contracts Violating Mandatory Legal Provisions

**Case Overview**: Company A and Company B entered into a contract stipulating that Company A would sell hazardous chemicals to Company B without obtaining the relevant permits.

**Court Decision**: The court determined the contract to be invalid. According to the "Safety Management Regulations for Hazardous Chemicals," enterprises dealing with hazardous chemicals must obtain the corresponding business permits. The contract violated mandatory legal provisions and was deemed invalid pursuant to Article 153 of the Civil Code.

**Legal Basis**: Article 153 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts violating mandatory provisions of laws and administrative regulations are invalid.

## Case 2: Contracts Entered into by Persons Without Civil Capacity

**Case Overview**: 14-year-old Xiao Ming purchased gaming equipment worth 50,000 yuan using his own New Year's money without the consent of his guardian.

**Court Decision**: The court determined the purchase contract to be invalid. According to the Civil Code, minors under 16 years of age have limited civil capacity and cannot independently engage in civil legal acts that are not commensurate with their age and intelligence.

**Legal Basis**: Article 146, Paragraph 1 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts performed by persons without civil capacity are invalid.

## Case 3: Contracts Entered into by Fraudulent Means

**Case Overview**: When selling a used car, Zhang deliberately concealed the fact that the car had been in a serious accident and had undergone major repairs, and Wang purchased the car without knowing these facts.

**Legal Analysis**: This contract is revocable. According to Article 148 of the Civil Code, where one party uses fraudulent means to cause the other party to act against their true intention, the defrauded party has the right to request the people's court or arbitration institution to revoke it.

**Legal Basis**: Article 148 of the Civil Code.

## Case 4: Contracts Violating Public Order and Good Morals

**Case Overview**: Parties A and B entered into a contract stipulating that B would introduce a potential spouse to A and facilitate their marriage, with A paying B an "introduction fee" of 100,000 yuan.

**Court Decision**: The court determined the contract to be invalid. Freedom of marriage is a fundamental principle in our country, and setting monetary conditions for establishing marital relationships violates public order and good morals.

**Legal Basis**: Article 153, Paragraph 2 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts violating public order and good morals are invalid.

## Case 5: Contracts Maliciously Colluding to Harm the Interests of Others

**Case Overview**: Debtor A maliciously colluded with third party B to enter into a real estate sale contract at an obviously unreasonable low price, attempting to evade debt obligations to creditor C.

**Court Decision**: The court determined the sale contract to be invalid. Parties A and B maliciously colluded to harm the legitimate rights and interests of creditor C.

**Legal Basis**: Article 154 of the Civil Code stipulates that civil legal acts where the actor maliciously colludes with the other party to harm the legitimate rights and interests of others are invalid.
